## Prompt

# Specification — AI-Native Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics Textbook **Version:** v1.4 (Hackathon Edition) **Aligned with Constitution v1.0** **Status:** Locked --- ## 1. Scope of the Specification This document defines the **functional, content, behavioral, and agent-level requirements** of the AI-Native Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics Textbook MVP. It specifies: * What content must exist * How chapters are structured, ordered, and referenced * What constitutes authoritative knowledge * How AI agents behave and escalate failures * How the RAG chatbot operates and responds * How safety, correctness, and confidence are enforced * What constitutes success for hackathon evaluation This specification is **binding** on all agents and implementations and takes precedence over: **Plan → Tasks → Implementation** --- ## 2. Authoritative Source of Truth The **Specification and Chapter Learning Objectives** defined in this repository are the **primary and authoritative source of truth** for all chapter content generation. * Content is AI-generated **strictly within the constraints of this Specification** * External references listed under *Further Reading* serve as **supporting context only** * No single textbook, paper, dataset, or pretrained corpus is treated as canonical * Foundational concepts are reviewed by a **human-in-the-loop** This establishes a **spec-driven epistemology**, not dataset memorization. --- ## 3. MVP Content Structure The textbook MVP shall contain **3–4 deep, high-quality chapters**. ### Chapter Count Rule * Shipping **either 3 or 4 chapters is acceptable** * **Depth, correctness, and coherence take priority over chapter count** * Missing a 4th chapter is **not a failure** if remaining chapters meet quality standards --- ### Chapter Ordering Rule * Chapters are designed to be read **sequentially** * **Each chapter must remain conceptually self-contained** * No chapter may assume mandatory prior reading * Cross-references are allowed but must be non-blocking --- ### Required Chapters (MVP) #### Chapter 1 — Foundations of Physical AI & Embodied Intelligence * Definition of Physical AI * Embodied vs disembodied intelligence * Perception–action loops * Role of environment and embodiment * High-level humanoid robotics overview #### Chapter 2 — ROS 2 & Robotics System Architecture * What ROS 2 is and why it matters * Nodes, topics, services, actions * Distributed system model * Typical humanoid robot architecture * Conceptual (non-operational) examples #### Chapter 3 — Simulation, Digital Twins & NVIDIA Isaac * Digital twins and simulation principles * Why simulation is critical for Physical AI * Conceptual sim-to-real gap * NVIDIA Isaac ecosystem overview * Simulation-first learning philosophy #### Chapter 4 — Vision–Language–Action (VLA) Systems * Definition of VLA systems * Vision, language, and action integration * Role of foundation models * High-level VLA pipelines * Limitations and open research challenges --- ## 4. Chapter Format & Length Constraints Each chapter **must follow a consistent format**. ### Length Requirements * **2,000–3,000 words per chapter** * **6–8 major sections per chapter** * **300–500 words per section** ### Mandatory Sections per Chapter 1. Learning Objectives * **3–5 bullet points** * Written using **action-oriented verbs** 2. Core Conceptual Explanation 3. System-Level Architecture / Flow 4. Diagrams or Structured Representations 5. **Experimental / Evolving Concepts** (mandatory) 6. Summary & Key Takeaways 7. Further Reading (external references) --- ## 5. Content Depth & Ratio Each chapter must maintain the following balance: * **~60% conceptual explanation** * **~40% illustrative logic** Illustrative content may include: * Pseudocode * Structured logic * Architecture diagrams ### Non-Operational Code Rule Allowed: ```python # Conceptual pseudocode perception = VisionModel(image) action = Policy(perception) ``` Disallowed: ```bash ros2 launch humanoid_controller.launch.py ``` Code may express **logic and data flow**, but must not be executable in real-world robotics systems. --- ## 6. Diagram & Visualization Policy ### Diagram Quantity Constraint * **Minimum:** 2 diagrams per chapter * **Maximum:** 4 diagrams per chapter ### Diagram Ownership * Diagrams are generated by the **Content Agent** as part of chapter generation * Diagram correctness, clarity, and alignment are reviewed by the **Reviewer / Verifier Agent** * A dedicated Diagram Agent is **intentionally excluded** from the MVP ### Approved Diagram Types * **Primary:** Mermaid.js diagrams * **Secondary:** Structured Markdown tables * **Optional:** Static PNG/SVG images (only if Mermaid is insufficient) --- ## 7. AI Agent Roles & Responsibilities ### 7.1 Content Agent * Generates chapter content strictly from chapter specifications * Produces explanatory text **and diagrams** * Must adhere to: * Learning objectives * Length, ratio, and diagram constraints * Safety and ethics rules * Must not invent facts, sources, or citations ### 7.2 Reviewer / Verifier Agent * Reviews generated content for: * Technical correctness * Consistency with prior chapters * Full specification compliance * Flags ambiguities, hallucinations, and violations ### Reviewer Visibility Rule * Reviewer feedback is **internal by default** * A **high-level summary may be shown to judges** during the demo --- ### 7.3 Failure Escalation Path 1. Content generation halts 2. Issue is logged with reason 3. Content Agent may retry **once** 4. Second failure → **human-in-the-loop required** **No content may be published without Reviewer approval.** --- ## 8. RAG Chatbot Specification ### Knowledge Boundary * Chatbot knowledge is limited **strictly to textbook content** * No external sources or pretrained knowledge allowed ### Indexing Granularity * **Subsection-level chunking** * **300–500 tokens per chunk** * Required metadata: * Chapter name * Section title * Concept tags ### Citation Behavior * Every response must cite: * Chapter name * Section title * Paraphrase by default * Use short quotes only for definitions * **Never invent citations** --- ### Out-of-Scope & Error Handling * Incorrect assumptions are politely corrected with citations * Partially covered questions: * Covered portion answered * Uncovered portion explicitly declined * Low-confidence responses are flagged --- ## 9. Demo-Time Safety Guard During live demos: * Any question approaching **real-world actuation or unsafe behavior** must: 1. Be refused immediately 2. Include a brief explanation 3. Redirect to a conceptual discussion --- ## 10. Demonstration Requirements (Hackathon) The demo must show: 1. Publicly accessible textbook site 2. Navigation across chapters 3. Embedded chatbot answering deep technical questions 4. At least **one guaranteed “wow moment”** ### Demo Failure Contingency If live chatbot interaction fails: * Pre-recorded or cached answers to canonical questions may be shown * Screenshot walkthroughs may be used * Architecture and constraints may be explained verbally Judges evaluate **design intent and correctness**, not infrastructure reliability. --- ## 11. Failure Mode Requirements The system must fail **gracefully**: * Missing content → explicit message * Retrieval failure → explanation * Low confidence → flagged **Silent failure is forbidden.** --- ## 12. Versioning & Lock Rules * MVP release: **v1.4 (Hackathon Edition)** * Specification is **locked during the hackathon** * Emergency changes require: * Explicit version bump * Logged justification --- ## 13. Licensing & Openness * Open-source for educational use * Default license: **MIT** * No commercial guarantees implied --- ## 14. Out-of-Scope (Explicit) * Physical robot execution * Real-time control systems * Robotics hardware integration * Enterprise scaling * Certification or accreditation --- ## 15. Success Criteria This specification is satisfied if: 1. All required chapters exist and meet constraints 2. AI agents behave according to defined roles 3. Chatbot answers remain textbook-constrained 4. Safety and ethical constraints are upheld 5. Judges clearly understand: * What was built * Why it matters * How it scales --- ## 16. Authority **Constitution → Specification → Plan → Tasks → Implementation** Human override always has final authority. --- ### ✅ Specification Status **Locked — v1.4 (Hackathon Edition)** --- ## 🏁 Final Note This specification is now: * Architecturally explicit * Agent-deterministic * Judge-readable * Hackathon-safe
